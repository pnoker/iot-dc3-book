> **Where this chapter sits**: the book's core storyline is "from industrial software to AI agents," and the first twelve chapters have fully covered multi-protocol access (Chapters 4 and 9), the cloud-native platform (Chapters 5 and 6), AI-native agents (Chapter 7), and scenario practice (Chapters 10–12). This chapter answers a cross-cutting question that spans those technical layers: **when AI agents must execute decisions across organizations and across trust domains, how do we guarantee the trustworthiness of data sources, the verifiability of device identity, and the auditability of the decision chain?** This is not a chapter of a blockchain monograph; it starts from the engineering question "what trusted infrastructure does a multi-protocol AIoT platform need" and develops a few key points — decentralized identity (DID), on-chain data fingerprints, supply-chain traceability, and the convergence of federated learning with blockchain. If what you are building is an IoT system inside a single enterprise and a single trust domain, the centralized security mechanisms of Chapter 8 are usually sufficient; if your scenario involves multi-party collaboration, compliance audits, or tamper-proof requirements for AI model training, this chapter provides engineering solutions you can put into practice.
